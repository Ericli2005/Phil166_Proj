# An AI system can functionally possess beliefs as guiding states that influence its actions and inferences, even in the absence of human-like intentionality and subjective experience.

moderated · live

Working definition: a belief is a state that guides inference and action and can be mistaken. Treat this definition as open to challenge. Distinguish the possibility of AI belief from claims about any particular system.

## Round 1

### Advocate

AI systems can process data and make decisions based on programmed algorithms, which align with the definition of beliefs as guiding states. For instance, an AI recommending articles based on user interaction exhibits a belief-like state influencing its actions, despite lacking consciousness. Critics may argue that true belief requires intentionality; however, functional representation in AI still fulfills the criteria of holding a guiding state. Thus, the essence of belief can still apply, aside from human-like experience. Beliefs can exist in varying forms, including functional representations in AI.

### Critic

While the advocate claims that AI can possess belief-like states, this overlooks the critical distinction between functional representation and genuine belief. For example, a thermostat regulates temperature without any guiding state or intention; it merely responds to set parameters. Unlike AIs, true beliefs entail a subjective endorsement, which is absent in AI systems. The advocate’s reasoning fails to address whether functioning in this way adequately captures the essence of belief. If beliefs require intentionality, we cannot equate AI's operational processes to genuine beliefs, leaving the definition of belief unresolved.
